Dame Agatha Mary Clarissa Christie, Lady Mallowan (née Miller; 15 September 1890 – 12 January 1976) was an English writer known for her detective novels. She is best known for her 66 detective novels and 14 short story collections, particularly those featuring the Belgian detective Hercule Poirot and the amateur sleuth Miss Marple.

She is the best-selling fiction writer of all time, with her novels having sold an estimated two billion copies. Her play The Mousetrap has been performed in London since 1952, the longest initial run of any play in history.

Early life

Agatha Miller was born in Torquay, Devon, the youngest of three children. She was largely educated at home and developed a love of reading and storytelling early on. In 1914 she married Archibald Christie, a Royal Flying Corps pilot. During the First World War she worked in a hospital dispensary, where she gained knowledge of poisons that later featured in her books.

Career

Her first novel, The Mysterious Affair at Styles (1920), introduced Hercule Poirot. In December 1926 she disappeared for eleven days, causing a national sensation before she was found at a hotel in Harrogate. She divorced in 1928 and in 1930 married archaeologist Max Mallowan, accompanying him on excavations in the Middle East, which inspired books such as Murder in Mesopotamia. She also wrote six romance novels under the name Mary Westmacott.

Notable works

  • Death on the Nile (1937)
  • And Then There Were None (1939)
  • The Murder at the Vicarage (1930) – first Miss Marple novel
  • The Mousetrap (play, 1952)

Legacy

Christie was made a Dame Commander of the Order of the British Empire in 1971. Her works have been adapted many times for film, television, radio and stage, and she remains one of the most widely read authors in the world.
